THE PROBLEM

The hardest part of getting protected is being seen getting it.

Pharmacy counters mean eye contact, questions and gossip. Clinics mean queues and judgement. So many young people simply go without.

AYHK takes the counter away. Someone walks up, taps, takes what they need and walks off, and the only thing recorded is that an item left the machine.

HOW IT WORKS

Three taps. Under a minute. Nobody watching.

01

Pick an item on the touchscreen. A one-tap Exit button clears the screen instantly.

PRIVACY BY DESIGN

We can't leak what we never collect.

There is no camera, no sign-up and no account. The kiosk logs only what the programme needs: which item type, which kiosk, and a rough time of day.

  • Built around Ghana's Data Protection Act, 2012 (Act 843)
  • Sponsored tokens carry no payment trail

THE HARDWARE

Engineered for the heat, the dust and the power cuts.

BRAIN

Raspberry Pi 4

Runs the touchscreen, sensors and payments in one small, repairable unit.

POWER

Solar + battery

A 20 W panel feeds a 12 V 20 Ah battery, so it keeps working when the grid doesn't.

SENSING

Know every drop

Weight and infrared slot sensors confirm delivery. A temperature sensor guards stock between 15 and 25 °C.

PAYMENTS

MoMo, Telecel Cash, AT Money, tokens

Tap-to-pay mobile money, MoMoPay references, and a QR and barcode scanner for sponsored tokens.

CONNECTIVITY

4G with Wi-Fi fallback

Records wait safely on the kiosk and sync to the partner dashboard whenever a connection is available.

SECURITY

Tamper detection

Reed switches raise an alert the moment a door is opened outside a restock visit.

QUESTIONS WE EXPECT

Straight answers.

Is it really anonymous?

The kiosk has no camera and asks for no name or phone number. If you pay with mobile money, your provider still holds a normal payment record, so for a fully trail-free option use a token sponsored by a school or clinic.

What does it dispense?

The pilot is designed around condoms, pregnancy tests and sanitary pads. The final list is agreed with each site's health partners.

Where can it go?

Anywhere young people already are: senior high schools, universities, youth centres and clinics in any of Ghana's 16 regions. It runs on solar power, so it does not need a reliable grid.

What happens in a power cut?

A 20 W solar panel and a 12 V 20 Ah battery keep the kiosk running. If the connection drops, records wait on the kiosk and sync when 4G or Wi-Fi is back.

Who sees the data?

Partners see totals only: item types, stock and time slots, by kiosk and region. There is no personal data to see. Data-sharing terms are agreed with each partner.

Is it approved to operate?

AYHK is a concept in pilot design. Regulatory and ethics review with the relevant Ghanaian authorities is part of the pilot plan before any public launch.
